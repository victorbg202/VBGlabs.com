Spain's 400 prefix for commercial calls: what businesses should review

VBG Labs · 21 October 2026

Since 17 October 2026, Spain’s 400 range has been the dedicated identifier for outbound commercial calls. This is more than a change to the number displayed to the recipient: businesses need to distinguish prospecting and sales offers from support connected with an existing contract.

A resolution published in Spain’s Official State Gazette on 16 April 2026 reserved the nine-digit 400 code for this purpose and allowed six months for implementation. On 16 October, the Ministry for Digital Transformation confirmed that businesses were expected to start using it the following day and that operators may block commercial traffic originating from other number ranges.

This guide turns the change into operational decisions. It is not legal advice: the precise scope depends on a business’s activity and size and, crucially, the actual purpose of each call.

What counts as a commercial call?

The Ministry’s official 400 range FAQ describes commercial calls as those made to interact with prospective customers and present an offer. Customer service instead deals with questions or incidents concerning an existing contract.

Purpose matters more than the team name or the software used. Consider three illustrative situations:

Situation Initial classification What to review
A team calls a business that is not yet a customer to offer a service Commercial 400 range and lawful basis for contact
A customer reports a fault with an existing service and the business calls back Customer service Approved support channel and case record
An additional product is offered during a renewal May have a commercial purpose Main purpose, context, lawful basis and script

Mixed cases need an internal rule. Law 10/2025 requires customer service to be kept separate from commercial activity and restricts offers made during an enquiry or complaint unless they directly relate to resolving it and improve the conditions or price. Renaming an activity in the CRM does not change the real purpose of the conversation.

A 400 number is outbound only

The 400 range can originate calls but cannot receive them. A recipient who tries to call back will not reach the business on that number. This changes the workflow:

  • the script must clearly identify the caller and business;
  • the website, email or follow-up message should provide a verifiable return channel;
  • the sales representative must record the next action instead of relying on a callback to the 400 number;
  • customer-service calls must retain a distinct contact route.

The Ministry FAQ says that service relating to existing contracts may use short numbers, 800 or 900 ranges and geographic numbers. Avoid reusing the 400 route for support, debt collection or administrative calls merely because it has already been configured.

Businesses do not obtain 400 numbers directly from the numbering register either. The resolution makes them available to public telephone operators and equivalent providers. The practical first step is to ask your telephony or hosted-PBX provider how it supplies the range, which caller ID recipients will see and how relevant records can be retained.

The number is not permission to call

Calling from a 400 number does not make a campaign lawful. The Spanish Data Protection Agency’s guidance on human-operated commercial calls explains that prior consent or another valid lawful basis is still required. Legitimate interests may apply in some circumstances where there is a previous contractual relationship, the data was obtained lawfully and the offer concerns the business’s own similar products or services. That is not a general licence to call any purchased or collected list.

At the start of the conversation, the caller must identify the business, state the commercial purpose and explain the right to object or withdraw consent. If an automated call plays a recorded message without human involvement, the AEPD requires prior consent.

A campaign record should therefore include the source of the data, the chosen lawful basis, the date of the latest relationship or consent where relevant, exclusions and every objection received. The prefix identifies the channel; it does not replace this evidence.

A seven-step implementation plan

1. Inventory every call flow

Look beyond the main switchboard. Include calls launched from the CRM, company mobiles, outsourced providers, contact centres, automated reminders and callbacks. The one-page process template helps capture the trigger, finish, data, owner and exceptions.

2. Classify each flow by purpose

Mark it commercial, customer service or pending a decision. Add a real example and the expected outcome. If one route mixes sales and support, separate queues, scripts or permissions before changing technology.

3. Confirm the scope that applies

The resolution expressly imposes an exclusivity requirement on businesses within the scope of Law 10/2025, which covers essential sectors and certain large businesses in other sectors. The Ministry’s operational announcement, however, presents use of the 400 range in general terms. A small business should not determine its position from a headline alone: check its activity, size, call type and provider instructions, and obtain professional advice where the boundary is unclear.

4. Configure the channel with the provider

Verify that the number presented starts with 400, is limited to commercial flows and does not replace the number used by support routes. Ask how failed calls, any recordings and record exports are handled. Switching on one caller-ID field in a PBX is not necessarily the entire migration.

5. Update data and scripts

In the CRM, separate the call purpose, contact source, lawful basis, objection status and next action. Our guide to setting up a small-business CRM explains how to avoid unused fields while preserving clear ownership.

Keep the opening script short: caller identity, business, purpose and the option to object. It should also explain how the recipient can continue the conversation through an inbound channel, because the 400 number cannot receive calls.

6. Test normal and exceptional cases

Make test calls to numbers on different networks, confirm the caller ID and validate the return channel. Simulate an objection, an invalid number, an unanswered call and a contact who is already a customer. Retain evidence of the tests and identify who approved the result.

7. Measure quality, not just volume

Track blocked calls, contacts without a documented basis, objections not propagated across systems and calls sent through the wrong queue. Conversion is a sales metric; on its own, it does not show that the workflow follows the rules.

Pre-campaign checklist

  • The purpose of the call is classified and documented.
  • The business has confirmed whether the 400 range applies to the flow.
  • The operator presents the 400 caller ID correctly for commercial calls.
  • Customer service retains a separate number and queue.
  • The data source and lawful basis can be traced.
  • The script identifies the business, purpose and right to object.
  • An inbound contact route exists because 400 numbers cannot receive calls.
  • Objections propagate to every relevant system and supplier.
  • Failure, absence and mixed-purpose cases have been tested.
  • One person owns the review of incidents.

The change is easier to manage as a small process, data and telephony project than as a simple number replacement. If you need to map call flows, separate the cases and turn them into requirements for a CRM or phone system, we can help you design an automation with clear controls.
