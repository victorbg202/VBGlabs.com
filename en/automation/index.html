AUTOMATION

Automate what's actually worth automating

Processes, documents, messages and repetitive tasks that can run on their own. The hard part isn't installing a tool — it's deciding well what's actually worth taking off your plate.

THE PRINCIPLE

Not everything should be automated

Some businesses come to us wanting to "automate everything," and once we look closely, the real problem is something else: a poorly defined process, information that lives nowhere in writing, or a task that only happens twice a month and isn't worth building anything for. Automating something that doesn't work well just makes it fail faster.

So before proposing any automation, we look at three things: how much time is actually being lost, how much error risk there is if it keeps being done by hand, and how much it would cost — in time and money — to build it properly. If the numbers don't work out, we'll tell you, even if you're willing to pay for it.

WHERE IT USUALLY PAYS OFF

Common cases we work on

We don't work from a fixed catalog of 'products.' Every automation is designed for your specific process, but these are the kinds of work that usually have the most to gain.

Document handling

Invoices, delivery notes or contracts that arrive by email and need sorting, filing or entering into a system.

Invoices and payments

From receiving them to flagging possible duplicates, approving them, and having tax reports ready.

Customer communication

Bringing WhatsApp, email and social messages into one place, with automatic replies for repeat questions.

Reminders and follow-ups

Appointments, pending payments or renewals that need a timely nudge, without anyone having to remember.

Sales follow-up

Knowing where every customer or deal stands without rebuilding the picture from memory each time.

Dashboards and reporting

Seeing how the business is doing at a glance, instead of building the report by hand every month.

AI assistants

A first point of contact that answers common questions and only escalates to a person when it should.

Tool synchronization

Getting the tools you already use to talk to each other, without copying data between them by hand.

Custom software

When no existing tool fits well enough and it's worth building something made specifically for you.

QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my process is worth automating?

If you do it more than ten times a month, if it's prone to human error, or if it depends on someone remembering, it's usually worth a look. If it happens once a month, probably not.

Do I need to change the tools I already use?

Usually not. We typically connect the tools you already have before suggesting new ones.

How long does it take to go live?

A one-off automation usually takes 1 to 3 weeks once the scope is defined. Advanced projects depend on how many processes are involved.

What happens if the process changes later?

We deal with it when it comes up. We always document how what we build works, so it can be adjusted without starting over.

Want to know what's worth automating in your business?

In a 15-20 minute conversation we can tell you if it makes sense, and where we'd start.
